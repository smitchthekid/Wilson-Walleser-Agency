# Find the Right SEO Tool

Most small businesses do not need a big SEO software bill. They need to know which job they are trying to do, and then pick the cheapest tool that does it well. This is how we think about it.

## Start with the job, not the tool

SEO tools tend to cover one or two of these jobs:

- **Keyword research:** finding the words your customers type into search.
- **Performance tracking:** seeing which pages and searches already bring people to your site.
- **Technical checks:** finding broken links, slow pages, and pages search engines cannot read.
- **Rank and visibility tracking:** watching where you show up over time, including in AI-generated answers.
- **Competitor and backlink research:** seeing who else is winning your searches and why.

If you cannot say which of these you need this month, you are not ready to pay for a tool yet.

## The free stack that covers most of it

Your own data is more useful than any estimate, and it costs nothing.

- **Google Search Console** shows the searches that already lead people to your site, the pages they land on, and where you rank. It is the best place to start, because it is real data about your site.
- **Google Analytics** shows what visitors do after they arrive. If you are new to it, our [Google Analytics metrics guide](/blog/google-analytics-metrics-for-beginners) explains the numbers worth watching.
- **Google Keyword Planner** lives inside Google Ads. It is free to use with an Ads account, and it gives search volume as ranges unless you are running active ad campaigns. Use it to compare ideas against each other, not as an exact count.
- **Google Trends** shows whether interest in a topic is rising, falling, or seasonal.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** gives you similar data for Bing search.
- **Google Business Profile insights** matter if you serve a local area.

One more free source: the words your customers use on sales calls and in emails. Those phrases are often better keywords than anything a tool suggests. Our post on [how people search for things online](/blog/how-humans-search-for-things-online-if-that-then-this) goes deeper on intent.

## Keyword research tools worth a look

When Search Console and Keyword Planner are not enough, a dedicated keyword tool helps you find related searches, compare difficulty, and see what competitors rank for.

- **Keywords Everywhere** is a browser extension that shows search data next to your Google results. It has moved to a paid, credit-based model (credits come with annual plans), so check its current pricing before relying on it.
- **Serpstat** offers keyword research, competitor analysis, and rank tracking, with free and paid plans. Check its current plans.
- **Ubersuggest** is a simple option for small sites. Free usage is limited, so check what you get before you commit.
- **Semrush and Ahrefs** are the large all-in-one platforms. They are strong, and for many small businesses they are more than you need.

A word of caution: every tool estimates search volume from its own data, so the numbers will not match. Treat them as a way to rank your ideas, not as exact traffic forecasts. When two tools disagree, check Search Console for what is actually happening on your site.

## Technical checks

A crawler visits your site the way a search engine does and flags problems: broken links, missing page titles, duplicate pages, slow templates. Screaming Frog is a widely used desktop crawler, and Search Console also reports indexing issues for free. For a small site, a crawl every few months is usually enough.

## Tracking visibility in AI answers

More people now get answers from Google's AI features, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. You can check how you appear without buying anything:

1. Write 15 to 20 questions a real customer might ask about your business or service.
2. Run them in each tool and note whether your business is mentioned, and which sources are cited.
3. Log the results in a spreadsheet and repeat monthly.

Some paid tools now automate this. The category is new and changing quickly, so look hard at what a tool actually measures before you subscribe.

## Using AI to speed up the work

An AI assistant can group a long keyword list by topic, draft content briefs, and sort searches by intent. It saves time, but it can also invent details. Have a person review the output, and confirm any volume or ranking figure in a real tool.

## What we would pick at each stage

| Stage | What we would use |
| --- | --- |
| Just starting out | Search Console, Analytics, Keyword Planner, Google Trends |
| Publishing regularly | The above, plus one keyword tool and an occasional site crawl |
| Competing in a crowded market | A full platform for competitor and backlink research, plus a monthly AI visibility check |

Add a paid tool only when a free one stops answering your question.
